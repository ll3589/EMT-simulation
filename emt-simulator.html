Before you meet Mia

What is EMT?

Enhanced Milieu Teaching (EMT) is a naturalistic teaching approach for promoting language and communication in everyday contexts. It's designed for children in the early stages of language learning — using single words or short phrases. EMT blends three things: arranging the environment to support communication, responding naturally to the child, and using targeted prompts to teach specific language.

Who is Mia?

Mia is a 4-year-old with a language delay. She uses 1–2 word approximations ("bock" for block, "mow" for more), communicates a lot through reaching and pointing, and loves blocks, trains, and toy animals. She gets frustrated when she can't communicate her needs. Your job is to follow her lead, respond to her communication, and use EMT strategies to support her language.

The 6 EMT strategies

1. Environmental Arrangement
Place preferred toys slightly out of reach. Offer choices. Create natural reasons for the child to communicate.
2. Responsive Interaction
Respond to ALL communication — gestures, sounds, words. Follow Mia's lead. Pause 3+ seconds before your next turn.
3. Target-Level Language
Model language at Mia's level or just above. Use short, grammatically complete sentences: "The block is big."
4. Toy Talk
Talk about the toy using its name. Describe its action, location, or quality: "The train is going fast."
5. Expansions
When Mia says a word, repeat it in a complete sentence: she says "bock" → you say "The block is heavy."
6. Milieu Prompts
Use sparingly (6–10 per session): Model ("say 'block'"), Mand-model ("what do you want?"), Time delay (expectant wait), Incidental teaching (prompt when child requests).
